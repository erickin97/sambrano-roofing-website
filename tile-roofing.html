Understanding Your Roof

The visible tile is only one part of the system

Concrete and clay tile are durable, attractive roofing materials common throughout Tucson and Southern Arizona. But the tile itself is really the outer layer of a multi-part system — underneath it is the underlayment, flashing, and decking that actually keep water out. A tile roof can look fine on the surface while the components underneath are aging.

Concrete & Clay Tile

Both are long-lasting roofing materials common in this region. Individual tiles can crack or break from impact or age and are typically repaired or replaced without disturbing the whole roof.

Underlayment

The waterproofing layer beneath the tile. It typically has a shorter service life than the tile itself, which is why it's inspected separately — see our dedicated Tile Underlayment page.

Flashing, Valleys & Penetrations

Flashing at valleys, walls, and roof penetrations (vents, skylights, HVAC) are common areas where water intrusion starts if details deteriorate or were not installed correctly.

Ridge Details & Decking

Ridge caps and the roof decking underneath everything are inspected as part of a full tile roof evaluation, particularly on older roofs or after storm exposure.

Roof-to-Wall Transitions

Where a tile roof meets a wall or parapet is another common area to check for proper flashing and sealant condition.

Arizona Exposure

Heat, sustained UV exposure, monsoon rainfall, and thermal cycling (repeated heating and cooling) all affect how a tile roofing system ages over time.

Tile Lift & Reset

What tile lift & reset means

When the underlayment beneath a tile roof needs attention but the tile itself is in usable condition, a full tear-off isn't always necessary. Tile lift & reset is a process where the existing tile is carefully lifted, the underlayment beneath it is addressed, and the tile is reset back into place.

Why tile can often be reused

Concrete and clay tile is a durable material, and tile in good physical condition can often be lifted, set aside, and reset once work on the underlayment is complete. Whether your existing tile is a good candidate depends on its actual condition at inspection — broken or badly worn tile may need to be replaced individually.

Why scope depends on your roof

Not every tile roof needs the same scope of work. The appropriate solution depends on the condition of the existing underlayment, decking, flashing, and tile — which is why we start with an inspection rather than a one-size-fits-all recommendation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Tile roofing questions from Tucson homeowners

What is tile lift and reset?

It's a process where existing roof tile is carefully lifted so the underlayment beneath it can be addressed, then the tile is reset back into place — often avoiding the need to source and install entirely new tile.

Can good tile be reused after underlayment work?

Often, yes, if the tile is in sound physical condition. Individual cracked or broken tiles are typically identified during inspection and replaced as needed.

How do I know if my tile roof needs attention?

Visible cracked or displaced tile, staining on interior ceilings, or a roof that hasn't been inspected in several years are all reasons to have a professional take a look. A tile roof can look fine from the ground while underlying components need attention.

Does every tile roof need underlayment replacement?

No. The appropriate scope of work depends on the actual condition found during inspection, not a blanket rule. See our Tile Underlayment page for more detail.
