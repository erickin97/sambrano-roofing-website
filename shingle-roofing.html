What to Look For

Signs a shingle roof may need attention

Shingle roofs age gradually, and several visible and hidden factors go into whether repair or replacement makes sense.

Curling & Brittleness

Shingles that curl at the edges or feel brittle to the touch are showing age-related wear that can reduce their weather resistance.

Granule Loss

Excessive granule loss (often visible as buildup in gutters) exposes the shingle's underlying material to more direct UV and weather exposure.

Missing or Wind-Damaged Shingles

Missing, lifted, or displaced shingles — common after Tucson's monsoon winds — leave the roof deck more exposed and are usually addressed as a priority repair.

Active Leaks

Interior staining or active leaks point to a failure somewhere in the roofing system — the shingles, flashing, or underlayment — and warrant a prompt inspection.

Flashing Issues

Deteriorated or improperly installed flashing around chimneys, vents, and valleys is a common source of leaks independent of the shingles themselves.

Roof-Deck Condition

What's found once shingles are removed — soft spots, rot, or water staining on the decking — can affect the scope of a repair or replacement project.

A Genuine Evaluation

Repair or replacement: a professional evaluation, not a sales pitch

Deciding between repair and replacement depends on several factors together, not any single issue in isolation: the roof's age, overall condition, extent of damage, history of previous repairs, and the condition of the underlayment, decking, and flashing. We do not recommend replacement for every older roof — a roof with isolated, repairable damage and a sound deck may only need targeted work.

When repair may make sense

Isolated damage, a limited number of affected shingles, and a roof deck and underlayment in reasonably sound condition can often be addressed with a targeted repair rather than full replacement.

When replacement may be appropriate

Widespread deterioration, repeated repairs to the same areas, damaged underlayment or decking found during inspection, or a roof nearing the end of its material's typical service life are all factors that can point toward replacement being the more appropriate option.

When Replacement Is Appropriate

What a new roof can address

When replacement is the appropriate option, it can offer some real, factual benefits — without guaranteeing outcomes we can't control.

New Materials

Replacement installs new roofing materials, addressing widespread age-related deterioration rather than patching around it.

Replacing Compromised Components

Damaged underlayment, decking, and flashing can be replaced as part of the project rather than left in place under new shingles.

Inspecting Concealed Components

Replacement provides an opportunity to inspect roof-deck and structural components that are normally hidden from view, and to address anything found before it's covered again.

Improved Appearance & Condition

A new roof improves the overall condition and appearance of the roofing system, installed according to manufacturer requirements for the selected material.

Our Process

From inspection to final walkthrough

01

Inspection

We assess the current condition of the shingles, flashing, and (where visible) underlayment and decking.

Frequently Asked Questions

Shingle roofing questions from Tucson homeowners

How do I know whether my shingle roof needs repair or replacement?

An inspection is the reliable way to know. We evaluate age, damage extent, prior repairs, and the condition of underlayment and decking together, rather than recommending replacement by default.

What happens if damaged decking is found?

Damaged sections of decking are repaired or replaced before new underlayment and shingles are installed — this is addressed as part of the process, not skipped.

Do I need a full roof replacement if I just have a few missing shingles?

Not necessarily. Isolated, limited damage can often be repaired. Replacement becomes more likely to be recommended when damage is widespread or underlying components are compromised.

How long does a shingle roof replacement take?

Timelines vary by roof size, scope of work, and weather — we'll give you a project-specific timeframe as part of your written estimate.
