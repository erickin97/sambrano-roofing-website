Why It Matters

Tucson's climate is hard on flat and low-slope roofs

Southern Arizona roofs deal with a combination most parts of the country don't: intense, sustained UV exposure, extreme surface heat, and the sudden, heavy rainfall of monsoon season. Over time, that combination can dry out and break down unprotected roofing surfaces, leading to cracking, granule loss, and reduced weather resistance.

UV Exposure

Constant sun exposure can accelerate the breakdown of roofing surfaces over time, which is part of why reflective, UV-resistant coatings are commonly used on Tucson flat roofs.

Surface Heat

Roof surface temperatures in Tucson summers can run well above ambient air temperature, which can accelerate wear on aging or unprotected roofing materials.

Monsoon Weathering

Heavy, fast-moving monsoon storms test a roof's waterproofing and drainage all at once — areas with existing wear or poor drainage are where problems tend to show up first.

Our Process

What a proper roof-coating job involves

A roof coating is only as good as the surface underneath it. Our process is built around inspecting and preparing the roof first — not just applying product.

01

Inspection

We inspect the existing roof surface, flashing, penetrations, and drainage to determine current condition and whether coating is an appropriate option.

02

Cleaning & Repairs

The surface is cleaned and any necessary repairs are made before coating — loose or damaged material, deteriorated seams, and problem areas around penetrations are addressed first.

03

Compatibility & Adhesion Check

We evaluate the existing roofing material — whether it's a built-up or modified bitumen system, or another substrate — to help confirm the coating system is compatible, which is an important factor in proper adhesion.

04

Drainage Review

We look at how water moves across the roof. Areas with ponding water are noted, since coating alone does not resolve underlying drainage problems.

05

Coating Application

The coating system is applied according to manufacturer specifications and product requirements for the roof type and conditions.

06

Maintenance & Recoating

Coatings are a maintenance item, not a one-time fix. We can discuss a maintenance and recoating schedule based on your roof's condition and exposure.

Important Considerations

Coating suitability depends on your roof's condition

Not every roof is a good candidate for coating. Whether coating is appropriate depends on the roof's substrate, existing condition, drainage, and compatibility with the coating system — a professional inspection is the only reliable way to determine that for your specific roof.

Coating may be appropriate when:

The existing roof substrate is structurally sound, drainage is functioning properly, and the surface is compatible with the coating system after proper preparation and repair.

Coating may not be appropriate when:

There is structural deterioration, severe substrate failure, major unresolved drainage or ponding issues, or an unsupported/compromised roof assembly. A coating is not designed to fix these underlying problems, and applying one over them is not a substitute for addressing the actual cause.

Frequently Asked Questions

Roof coating questions from Tucson homeowners

Can every roof be coated?

No. Coating suitability depends on the roof's substrate, existing condition, drainage, and compatibility with the coating system. An inspection can help determine whether coating is an appropriate option for your specific roof.

When should a roof coating be maintained or recoated?

This depends on the coating product used, the roof's exposure, and its condition over time. We can review your roof's condition and discuss an appropriate maintenance timeline during an inspection.

What does surface preparation involve?

Preparation typically includes cleaning the roof surface, repairing damaged or deteriorated areas, and confirming the substrate is suitable for the coating system before application.

Does a roof coating fix leaks?

A coating is designed to provide a protective, waterproofing surface layer when properly installed and maintained, but it is not a substitute for repairing structural damage, severe substrate failure, or unresolved drainage problems. Those issues need to be addressed directly.

For more on ponding water, drainage, and when a coating repair is the right call, see our Roofing Knowledge Center. If inspection finds a problem a coating alone can't address, see Roof Repairs or, for an active leak, Leak Repairs.
